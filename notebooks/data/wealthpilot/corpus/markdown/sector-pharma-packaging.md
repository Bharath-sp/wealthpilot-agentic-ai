### SECTION 8.14: SECTOR UNDERWRITING NOTE — PHARMACEUTICAL PACKAGING SMEs

#### 8.14.1 Scope & Applicability
This underwriting note governs all direct balance-sheet lending, term loans, and working capital facilities extended to Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) engaged in primary, secondary, and tertiary packaging manufacturing for the pharmaceutical industry. Applicable NACE/SIC codes include primary blister foil convertors, high-density polyethylene (HDPE) bottle blow-molders, glass vial convertors, and specialized printed carton manufacturers.

---

#### 8.14.2 Maximum Sector Portfolio Exposure Cap
1. **Total Book Limit:** The aggregate exposure to the Pharmaceutical Packaging SME sector shall not exceed **6.5%** of Ashva Capital’s total outstanding loan book at any point in time. 
2. **Single Borrower Group Cap:** Exposure to a single borrower group in this sector is capped at **0.8%** of the total loan book or **INR 180,000,000**, whichever is lower.
3. **Breach Protocol:** If sector exposure reaches **6.0%**, underwriters must escalate all new applications to the Chief Credit Officer. Applications received after reaching the 6.5% cap shall be automatically declined without credit review.

---

#### 8.14.3 Working Capital Cycle Baseline (118 Days)
Underwriters shall benchmark cash conversion models against a standard sector working capital cycle of **118 days**, broken down as follows:

*   **Raw Material Inventory (Polymer granules, aluminum foil, specialty paperboard):** 35 days.
*   **Work-In-Progress (WIP) & Cleanroom Quarantine Hold:** 18 days.
*   **Finished Goods Hold (Awaiting client Quality Assurance release):** 20 days.
*   **Receivables Realization (Debtors):** 45 days.

*Mandatory Adjustment:* If an applicant’s historical Receivables Realization exceeds **65 days**, the underwriter must apply an automatic **15% haircut** to the eligible borrowing base for working capital line calculations.

---

#### 8.14.4 Sector-Specific DSCR Floor
1. **Minimum DSCR Floor:** **1.35x** (Calculated as EBITDA / [Annual Interest + Scheduled Principal Repayments]). 
   *   *Note:* This replaces Ashva Capital’s standard baseline DSCR floor of **1.25x**. The higher threshold reflects non-negotiable operational risks associated with client plant audit delays and batch rejection downtime.
2. **Stressed DSCR Threshold:** Under a simulated **12% revenue drop** and a **200 bps increase** in variable input costs (raw polymer/foil prices), the stressed DSCR must not fall below **1.12x**.

---

#### 8.14.5 Seasonality Adjustments & Liquidity Buffers
Pharma packaging cash flows exhibit structural seasonal troughs between January 1 and March 31 (Q1) due to client annual maintenance shutdowns, drug master file (DMF) updates, and mandatory ISO 15378 cleanroom re-validations.

1. **Q1 Revenue Haircut:** Underwriters shall apply a **15% revenue reduction** to historical Q1 baseline cash flow projections when sizing debt service capacity.
2. **Debt Service Reserve Account (DSRA):** Borrowers are required to build a dedicated DSRA equivalent to **60 days of principal plus interest** between October 1 and November 30 each year. This reserve shall remain locked in an escrow account with Ashva Capital until April 30 of the following year.

---

#### 8.14.6 Primary Causes of Sector Distress
Underwriters must evaluate and document specific mitigation for the two most prevalent drivers of default in this sector:

1. **Batch Contamination & Regulatory Suspension (Cleanroom Integrity Failure):**
   *   *Driver:* Failure to maintain ISO Class 7 or Class 8 cleanroom standards leads to particulate/microbial contamination, resulting in immediate product recalls, customer WHO-GMP audit failures, and 60-to-180-day operational suspension.
   *   *Required Assessment:* Verification of continuous environmental monitoring logs for the preceding 12 months and proof of active Product Contamination / Recall Liability Insurance with a minimum cover of **INR 50,000,000**.
2. **Client Concentration Shock & Formulation Shift:**
   *   *Driver:* High dependence on a limited number of pharmaceutical formulation manufacturers. The loss of a key client or a client's transition to alternative delivery systems (e.g., shifting from blister packs to pre-filled syringes) eliminates revenues faster than fixed overheads can be reduced.
   *   *Required Assessment:* Enforce concentration limits per Clause 8.14.8.

---

#### 8.14.7 Acceptable Collateral Types & LTV Limits

| Collateral Category | Specific Acceptable Assets | Maximum LTV Limit | Valuation Requirements |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Class A** | High-precision cleanroom machinery (e.g., German/Japanese injection blow-molding machines, cold-form blister lines) under 7 years of age. | **65%** | Liquidation value based on an independent appraisal from an Ashva-approved empaneled engineer. |
| **Class B** | Trade Receivables from Tier-1 formulation exporters holding active USFDA or EU-GMP approvals, aged < 60 days. | **80%** | Subject to direct tri-party escrow or notice of assignment. |
| **Class C** | Freehold industrial land and buildings housing certified ISO 15378 cleanroom facilities. | **70%** | Lower of two independent valuations. |

**Strictly Unacceptable Collateral:**
*   Inventories of custom-printed foil, custom-debossed cartons, or client-branded blister films (Zero realizable scrap value; mandatory 100% haircut).
*   Machinery older than **10 years** or machinery located outside certified cleanroom environments.
