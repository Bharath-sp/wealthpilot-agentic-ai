# Sector Underwriting Note: Light Engineering

# ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL
## SECTION 4: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING GUIDELINES
### 4.12 Sub-Sector: Light Engineering SME Lending

#### 4.12.1 Purpose and Scope
This policy governs credit assessment, structuring, and risk acceptance criteria for small and medium-sized enterprises operating within the light engineering sector. Underwriters must apply these parameters strictly. Any deviation requires formal sign-off from the Credit Committee, chaired by Chief Credit Officer Marcus Vance.

#### 4.12.2 Working Capital Cycle
1. The typical working capital cycle for light engineering SMEs—measured from inventory purchase to cash collection—is **75 days**.
2. Underwriters must base cash flow projections on a 75-day operational cycle unless historical financials for the preceding 24 months demonstrate a verified variance of greater than 10 days. 
3. Accounts receivable aging schedules must be obtained monthly. Receivables exceeding **90 days** from invoice date are deemed ineligible for drawing base calculations.

#### 4.12.3 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Floor
1. Due to high equipment replacement expenditures and raw material price volatility, the sector-specific DSCR floor for light engineering SME lending is **1.35x**.
2. This supersedes the general commercial lending baseline of 1.25x. 
3. The 1.35x DSCR must be maintained under base-case projections and a mandatory 15% revenue stress test.

#### 4.12.4 Seasonality Adjustments
1. Light engineering borrowers experience a pronounced seasonal peak from **January to March**, driven by contract finalizations and industrial supply replenishment cycles.
2. Underwriters must evaluate historical cash flows over a full 12-month trailing period to capture off-peak troughs (historically July to September).
3. Working capital facilities may be structured with seasonal limit escalations of up to **20%** above baseline during the January to March window, provided the borrower maintains a minimum unencumbered liquidity buffer equal to **1.5 times** the monthly debt service obligation during off-peak quarters.

#### 4.12.5 Primary Causes of Distress
Underwriters must specifically address the mitigation of the two primary causes of financial distress identified in this sector:
1. **Machine Obsolescence and Maintenance Delays:** Sudden capital expenditure requirements for specialized CNC, lathe, or stamping machinery failures without adequate reserve funding. Mitigation requires a mandatory maintenance reserve account funded at **2%** of gross annual revenue.
2. **Raw Material Price Volatility:** Unhedged exposure to primary metals (steel, aluminum, copper) squeezing gross margins. Mitigation requires fixed-price supply contracts of at least **90 days** duration or pass-through pricing clauses in primary customer contracts.

#### 4.12.6 Collateral and Security Requirements
Acceptable collateral types and maximum advance rates are restricted to:
1. **First-ranking charge over plant and machinery:** Maximum advance rate of **65%** of forced sale value (FSV) as determined by an independent Ashva Capital-approved panel valuer.
2. **First-ranking hypothecation of book debts and inventory:** Maximum advance rate of **70%** for raw materials and finished goods, and **50%** for work-in-progress (WIP).
3. **Collateral management agreements (CMA)** are mandatory for inventory values exceeding **500,000 USD** equivalent.

#### 4.12.7 Portfolio Concentration Limits
1. Total credit exposure to the light engineering sector shall not exceed **14 percent** of the total Ashva Capital loan book.
2. Current sector exposure stands at **11.5 percent**, leaving an available sector headroom of **2.5 percent**.
3. New loan originations in this sector that would breach the 14% ceiling are automatically rejected by the underwriting system without manual override capability.

#### 4.12.8 Tenor Limits
1. The maximum allowable loan tenor for light engineering SME facilities is **72 months**, inclusive of any initial moratorium period.
2. Moratoriums on principal repayment are restricted to a maximum of **6 months** and apply solely to term loans funding machinery acquisition.
