# Sector Underwriting Note: Pharma Packaging

**ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 14: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING MANDATES**
**SUB-SECTION 14.4: PHARMA PACKAGING SME LENDING**

---

### 1.0 PURPOSE AND APPLICABILITY
This sub-section establishes the mandatory underwriting rules for all Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the Pharmaceutical Packaging sector (NAICS/SIC equivalent codes related to converted paper product manufacturing, plastic packaging film, and pharmaceutical-grade blister foil manufacturing). Underwriters deviating from these clauses without prior sign-off from the Credit Risk Committee under Clause 8.2 will trigger an automatic compliance audit.

### 2.0 WORKING CAPITAL CYCLE AND CASH FLOW DYNAMICS
2.1 **Operating Cycle Benchmarks:** The typical working capital cycle for approved entities in the Pharma Packaging sector is **60 days**, measured from raw material intake (food/medical grade polymers, aluminum foil, PVC/PVDC resins) to cash collection from finished goods delivery.
2.2 **Inventory Holding Requirements:** Underwriters must ensure borrowers maintain a minimum raw material inventory holding equivalent to 30 days of projected production to insulate against supply chain shocks of specialized polymers.
2.3 **Receivables Aging:** Accounts receivable exceeding 90 days past invoice date shall be excluded from the borrowing base calculation during asset-backed lending reviews.

### 3.0 DEBT SERVICE COVERAGE RATIO (DSCR) FLOOR
3.1 **Standard Override:** Notwithstanding the baseline Ashva Capital SME credit policy DSCR floor of 1.25x, all facilities extended within the Pharma Packaging sector are subject to a mandatory minimum **DSCR floor of 1.30x**.
3.2 **Calculation Basis:** DSCR shall be calculated as Net Operating Income divided by total debt service (principal and interest commitments) over a rolling 12-month historical and 12-month projected period, incorporating a mandatory 10% haircut on projected EBITDA to account for raw material price volatility.

### 4.0 SEASONALITY ADJUSTMENTS
4.1 **Demand Profile:** The Pharma Packaging sector exhibits **no marked season**; demand remains stable across all four fiscal quarters due to non-discretionary pharmaceutical manufacturing schedules.
4.2 **Working Capital Facilities:** Revolving lines of credit must not be structured with seasonal limit step-ups. If a borrower requests a temporary facility increase exceeding 15% of the sanctioned limit, the underwriter must demand a 90-day cash flow audit certified by an independent auditor appointed from the Ashva Capital panel (list maintained by Chief Risk Officer Marcus Vance).

### 5.0 SECTOR-SPECIFIC DISTRESS INDICATORS
Underwriters must specifically evaluate historical financials for the two primary structural causes of distress unique to this sector:
5.1 **Polymer and Aluminum Price Volatility:** Unhedged exposure to raw material price fluctuations (specifically low-density polyethylene and pharmaceutical-grade aluminum foil) that compress gross margins below 22%. Facilities where historical gross margins have dropped by more than 400 basis points over a rolling 24-month period shall be declined outright.
5.2 **Regulatory Compliance and Contamination Failures:** Loss of ISO 15378 certification (Primary packaging materials for medicinal products) or failure of Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) audits by major pharmaceutical clients. Any borrower experiencing a client contract termination exceeding 10% of total top-line revenue due to quality non-compliance is deemed in technical default of sector viability norms.

### 6.0 ACCEPTABLE COLLATERAL TYPES
Collateral valuation must apply the following mandated advance rates against forced liquidation value (FLV):
6.1 **Machinery and Equipment:** Dedicated pharmaceutical blister-forming lines, rotogravure printing presses, and slitter-rewind machines. Maximum advance rate: 55% of appraised FLV, backed by an independent valuation report not older than 180 days.
6.2 **Commercial Real Estate:** Factory premises and associated warehousing zoned for industrial manufacturing. Maximum advance rate: 70% of market value, subject to a first-ranking legal mortgage.
6.3 **Book Debts / Receivables:** Trade receivables from tier-1 and tier-2 pharmaceutical manufacturers only. Maximum advance rate: 75% of eligible receivables under 90 days. Unbilled revenues and advances to suppliers are strictly ineligible.

### 7.0 PORTFOLIO CONCENTRATION LIMITS
7.1 **Book Exposure Cap:** Total aggregate credit exposure to the Pharma Packaging sector shall not exceed **8 percent of the total Ashva Capital loan book**. 
7.2 **Current Utilization:** As of audit cycle Q3, current portfolio exposure stands at **6.4 percent** of the loan book, leaving a residual sector headroom of 1.6 percent. Underwriters must verify current sector exposure via the automated credit risk dashboard prior to issuing any Sanction Letter. Any application that would breach the 8 percent cap requires unanimous written approval from Chief Credit Officer Sarah Lin and Chief Executive Officer David Thorne.

### 8.0 FACILITY PARAMETERS AND TENOR LIMITS
8.1 **Maximum Tenor:** The maximum allowable repayment tenor for term loans extended to this sector is **60 months** (inclusive of any initial principal moratorium, which shall not exceed 6 months). Working capital demand loans and revolving credit facilities are subject to annual review and mandatory renewal windows.
8.2 **Exception Protocol:** Any waiver requests concerning the 1.30x DSCR floor, the 60-month maximum tenor, or the 60-day working capital baseline must be submitted via Form CP-902 to the Underwriting Exception Committee. No single underwriter possesses delegated authority to override sector-specific quantitative thresholds.
